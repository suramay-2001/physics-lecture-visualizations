**2 Formalism** (all `phase: 'books'`, Ground only)
- `l1-quantized:b5c` (derivation; Townsend Sec. 1.1, pp. 1-3, Eqs. 1.1-1.4). Gist: a charge circling an orbit has a moment proportional to its angular momentum, $\mu = \tfrac{q}{2m}L$ in SI; spin follows the same pattern with a factor $g \approx 2$ for the electron; a field gradient pushes with $F_z \simeq \mu_z\,\partial B_z/\partial z$. Silver's single electron gives $\mu_B = e\hbar/2m_e$ ($9.274\times10^{-24}$ J/T, claim `l1TnMuB`). New gloss: `g-factor`, `bohr-magneton` (first here). **Rosetta**: Townsend writes Gaussian units, so a $c$ sits in every formula and S names spin; the app uses SI and drops $c$ (his footnote 1, p. 2).
  Derivation (Ground steps, 4; result `F_z \simeq \mu_z\,\partial B_z/\partial z`): (1) $I = q/T,\ T = 2\pi r/v$ [view A]; (2) $\mu = IA = \tfrac{q}{2m}L$ [A]; (3) $\vec\mu = g\tfrac{q}{2m}\vec S$, $g \approx 2$ [view B]; (4) $F_z \simeq \mu_z\,\partial B_z/\partial z$ [B]. View A = `lab-r3` `{field:'uniform'}` (precession glint, no split); view B = `lab-r3` `{field:'gradient', model:'quantum'}` (two spots).
- `l1-sequential:b5a` (Townsend Sec. 1.2, Exp. 4, pp. 7-9, Figs. 1.4-1.6). Gist: replace the x magnet by his three-magnet device that recombines both beams without recording the path; then every atom comes out up again, as if no x device were there; blocking either path restores Experiment 3's 50/50. The chances alone cannot explain this; amplitudes add before squaring (p. 9). Teaches only the experimental fact; the derivation is Unit 3.5 (link forward in words). Stage: visual V1.1.
- `l1-average:b4a` (Townsend Sec. 1.4, p. 17). Gist: with N readings the up-count scatters by about $\sqrt{N}$-sized amounts; 55 up of 100 is unremarkable, 550 000 up of $10^6$ would be alarming. We put numbers on it: the exact scatter of the count is $\sqrt{N/4}$ (5 and 500), so 55 of 100 is 1 sigma and 550 000 of $10^6$ is 100 sigma (claims `l1TnZ55`, `l1TnZ550k`). **Rosetta**: his "$\sqrt N$" is an order of magnitude; the exact scatter for a 50/50 split is half of it. Stage: `lab-r3` `{readouts:['sigma-band'], batches:[100, 100, 100]}` (existing).
- `l1-vectors:b5a` (Townsend Sec. 1.3, pp. 10-13, Eqs. 1.5-1.7, 1.11, 1.14-1.15, Fig. 1.7). Gist: a field is told by its components $E_x = \hat i\cdot\mathbf E$; a spin state is told the same way, $c_\pm = \langle\pm z|\psi\rangle$; the bra is the ket with every number conjugated; the two chances then add to one. Numbers for the state $\cos25^\circ|{+z}\rangle + \sin25^\circ|{-z}\rangle$: $c_+ = 0.906$, chances 0.821 and 0.179 (claims `l1TnC1`, `l1TnP1`, `l1TnP2`, `l1TnSum`). **Rosetta**: his $c_\pm$ are the lecture's $\alpha,\beta$. Stage: `hilbert-plane` `{psi:{planeDeg:25}, basis:'z', shadows:true, rightAngle:true}` (existing; no derivation).

**2 Formalism**
- `l2-inner-product:b7a` (Townsend Sec. 2.1, pp. 29-32, Eqs. 2.3-2.11, 2.17-2.19). Gist: keep only the components and a ket is a column, its bra the conjugate row, and the inner product is row times column; for $|{+y}\rangle = (1, i)/\sqrt2$ the row $(1, -i)/\sqrt2$ gives 1, while forgetting the conjugate, $(1, +i)/\sqrt2$, gives 0 (claims `l2TnYYright` = 1, `l2TnYYwrong` = 0). **Rosetta**: his arrow with "S_z basis" under it is the lecture's "=" for a column in the z basis. Stage: `complex-plane` `{chain:{phasesDeg:[0, 180], sizes:[0.5, 0.5]}}` (wrong row: two products 1/2 and -1/2 cancel) then `{chain:{phasesDeg:[0, 0], sizes:[0.5, 0.5]}}` (right row: they add to 1).
- `l2-inner-product:b7b` (Townsend Sec. 2.1, pp. 31-33, Eqs. 2.12-2.15, 2.21-2.24). Gist: demand $\langle{-x}|{+x}\rangle = 0$ and unit length; this fixes $|{-x}\rangle$ as $(1, -1)/\sqrt2$ up to an overall phase $e^{i\delta}$, which he sets to 1. The same ket $|{+x}\rangle$ is the column $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ in the z basis and $(1, 0)$ in the x basis: same state, new coordinates (claims `l2TnMinusXDot` = 0, `l2TnMinusXNorm` = 1, `l2TnPlusXinX` = 1). Stage: `hilbert-plane` `{psi:'+x', basis:'x', shadows:true}` (existing).
- `l2-plus-y:b7a` (derivation; Townsend Sec. 1.5, pp. 18-19, Eqs. 1.23-1.28). Gist: give both kets arbitrary phases, pull each overall phase out front, and the chance that an $|{+x}\rangle$ atom reads $+y$ is $\tfrac12[1+\cos(\delta-\gamma)]$ where $\delta,\gamma$ are the two relative phases; only $\delta-\gamma = \pm\pi/2$ gives 1/2, so the factor $i$ is forced (claims `l2TnPy0` = 1, `l2TnPyQuarter` = 0.5, `l2TnPyPi` = 0). **Rosetta**: his $\delta_\pm,\gamma_\pm$ and relative phases $\delta,\gamma$ are the lecture's phase of $c$; his choice $\delta = 0$ is "real $|{+x}\rangle$".
  Derivation (Ground steps, 4; result `P = \tfrac12\,[1 + \cos(\delta-\gamma)]`): (1) $|{+x}\rangle = \tfrac{e^{i\delta_+}}{\sqrt2}[|{+z}\rangle + e^{i\delta}|{-z}\rangle]$, same for $|{+y}\rangle$ with $\gamma$ [view A]; (2) $\langle{+y}|{+x}\rangle = \tfrac{e^{i(\delta_+-\gamma_+)}}{2}[1 + e^{i(\delta-\gamma)}]$ [A]; (3) $|\cdot|^2 = \tfrac14(1 + e^{i\Delta})(1 + e^{-i\Delta})$ [view B]; (4) $\tfrac14(1 + e^{i\Delta})(1 + e^{-i\Delta}) = \tfrac12[1 + \cos(\delta-\gamma)]$, $\Delta = \delta-\gamma$ [B]. Views = the two halves of visual V2.1.
- `l2-three-bases:b6a` (Townsend Sec. 1.4, p. 15, Eqs. 1.17-1.19; Sec. 1.6 p. 25, Eq. 1.50). Gist: the z chances (1/2, 1/2) cancel every phase, so they cannot tell $|{+x}\rangle$ from $|{+y}\rangle$; the phase is real physics only through the other bases. Numbers for a phase of 40 degrees: z chance 0.50, x chance 0.883, y chance 0.821 (claims `l2TnPz40`, `l2TnPx40`, `l2TnPy40`). Stage: visual V2.2.

**2 Formalism**
- `l3-eigen:b6a` (derivation; Townsend Sec. 2.2, pp. 34-35, Eqs. 2.27-2.28). Gist: the bra of $\hat R|{+z}\rangle$ cannot be $\langle{+z}|\hat R$; two quarter turns make a half turn, which sends $|{+z}\rangle$ to $|{-z}\rangle$ and gives 0 where the length should be 1; the fix is the adjoint, $\langle{+x}| = \langle{+z}|\hat R^\dagger$, with $\hat R^\dagger\hat R = 1$ (claims `l3TnNaiveBra` = 0, `l3TnAdjBra` = 1, `l3TnRotIsX` = 1). **Rosetta**: his $\hat R(\tfrac\pi2\mathbf j)$ is the app's $R_y(90^\circ)$; his "adjoint" is the lecture's Hermitian conjugate; links forward in words to "unitary" in Lecture 5.
  Derivation (Ground steps, 4; result `\langle{+z}|\hat R^\dagger\hat R|{+z}\rangle = 1`): (1) naive bra: $\langle{+x}|{+x}\rangle = \langle{+z}|\hat R\hat R|{+z}\rangle$ [view A]; (2) $\hat R\hat R$ is the half turn $\hat R(\pi\mathbf j)$ and $\hat R(\pi\mathbf j)|{+z}\rangle = |{-z}\rangle$ [view B]; (3) so the naive length is $\langle{+z}|{-z}\rangle = 0$, not 1 [B]; (4) with $\hat R^\dagger$: $\langle{+z}|\hat R^\dagger\hat R|{+z}\rangle = 1$ [A]. View A = `hilbert-plane` `{psi:'+z', basis:'z', image:{matrix:[['sqrt(2)/2','-sqrt(2)/2'],['sqrt(2)/2','sqrt(2)/2']], label:'$\hat R|\psi\rangle$'}, shot:'H-FLAT'}` (arrow at 45 degrees = $|{+x}\rangle$); view B = the same with `image:R_IMG` (the half turn already in `L3.story.ts`, arrow at 90 degrees).
- `l3-projectors:b5a` (Townsend Sec. 2.3, pp. 42-44, Eqs. 2.46-2.48, 2.53, fn. 7). Gist: the identity can be built from any complete pair: $|{+x}\rangle\langle{+x}| + |{-x}\rangle\langle{-x}| = 1$ is the same operator as the z sum; a projector also acts on a bra, $\langle\psi|\hat P_+ = \langle\psi|{+z}\rangle\langle{+z}|$. Numbers: the x sum minus the identity is the zero matrix, the trace of one projector is 1 (claims `l3TnIdX`, `l3TnPxTrace`). **Rosetta**: his $\hat P_\pm$ is the lecture's $\hat P_{\pm z}$ (already named in Unit 4.2). Stage: visual V3.3.
- `l3-postulates:b6a` (Townsend Sec. 1.6, pp. 22 and 25, Eq. 1.50, Problem 1.13 idea).
