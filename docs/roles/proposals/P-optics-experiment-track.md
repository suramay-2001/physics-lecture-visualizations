**Page voice: a run sheet.** Each section answers four questions in this order, as plain paragraph cues the page itself uses: *Set* (what you dial: detuning, power, waist, exposure), *Measure* (what you read: atoms, counts, a histogram), *What goes wrong* (the limit that stops you pushing the dial), *How you know* (the number that checks it, computed here or reported by a paper, with the two printed side by side). Try-it paragraph = one `optics-bench` preset, as O1–O6. Format otherwise exactly O1–O6: `collection: 'optics'`, `length: 'full'` (900–1,500 words, target 1,000), sentences ≤ 40 words, paragraphs ≤ 120 words, section = one topic with a printed-source cue, every displayed number a keyed claim `o7…o11` with an engine call and a numpy twin, a section links ≥ 1 built unit (W4).
**Numbers rule (judge's batch rule).** *Computed* = engine, keyed claim, numpy twin. *Reported* = printed exactly as the source prints it, with paper and location, in the existing paragraph "reported" treatment (hollow point in a `plot` points figure, "Reported" badge in the aside); never typed as if computed. ## 2. O7 — From vapour to a cold cloud (links back: O1, O2, O3, O5)

**Apparatus.** An electrically heated dispenser feeds a 2D-MOT that pushes a beam into a glass cell; six beams and a field gradient make the 3D MOT; the field is switched off for a polarization-gradient step; the tweezers are already on. **Measure.** Atoms in the cloud (fluorescence), then the temperature of what the tweezers keep. **Try it.** `mot-load`, `pgc-window`.
- `o7-sequence` *The order of the run.* Source, MOT, sub-Doppler step, overlap with the tweezers, loading, image, rearrangement, readout (words and a numbered list; durations as reported). *Set:* nothing yet. *What goes wrong:* a shot is dominated by cooling and clean-up and the image, not by gates (our reading of the printed steps; the paper itself notes its clean-up is not optimised). *Reported (Manetsch, Methods "Loading single atoms in tweezers"):* MOT about 10⁷ atoms in 100 ms; 3D PGC 40 ms then a 40 ms wait; image 80 ms. Computed: `o7IsatCs` 1.10 mW/cm² (πhcγ/3λ³ [o7IsatCs]; the paper prints about 1.1 mW/cm² for the stretched state, shown beside it), cites `o3CsTD`, `o3CsTR`. Link `o1-compensation` (no oven or slower here, and why), `qc-o-q25-cooling` (new row).
- `o7-mot` *Filling the trap.* The cloud grows as N(t) = Rτ(1 − e^{−t/τ}), the rate balance of capture and loss; the force of §5.2 (O5) gives a spring and a damping. *Set (inputs from Manetsch):* gradient 20 G/cm, δ = −3.17 γ, total intensity 10 I₀ over six beams (s = 10/6 per beam, our reading), 2.5 cm beams. *Measure:* atoms against time. *What goes wrong:* the cloud is larger than the linear picture says because light scattered by atoms pushes them apart (words, from O5 §variants): the engine's Zeeman half-size is shown beside the reported 1.6 mm cloud, no verdict. *Claims:* `o7MotBetaK2` 0.0230 ħk² (`motLinear`, [o7MotBetaOverHbark2]), `o7MotOmegaHz` 190 Hz [o7MotOmega], `o7MotZeemanMm` 5.93 mm [o7MotZeemanSize], `o7CaptureV` 53.8 m/s over 2.5 cm (`capture`; ODE stop distance 2.5 cm checks it) [o7CaptureV], `o7Load2Tau` 0.865 and `o7Load95` 3.00 τ (`mot-load`) [o7Load2tau, o7Load95]. *Reported:* about 10⁷ atoms, 1.6 mm cloud (Manetsch, same Methods paragraph); Rb about 10⁷ atoms in 80 ms (Chiu et al., arXiv:2506.20660, Methods "MOT & Lattice Loading").
- `o7-pgc` *Below the Doppler limit, on purpose.* Turn off the field, raise the detuning to −19.5 γ, lower the intensity to 7 I₀ for 40 ms (reported): the Sisyphus scale of O3 at these settings. *Claims (O3's model, C² = 1, a scale not a prediction, stated):* `o7U1Pgc` −7.51 µK, `o7TlimLin` 0.939 µK and `o7TlimSig` 0.728 µK (`lightShiftGround`, `subDopplerLimit`), `o7DopplerOverTlim` 134 [o7U1Pgc, o7TlimLin, o7TlimSig, o7DopplerOverTlim]. *Reported:* Cs below 5 µK after red PGC and a Raman gray molasses (Graham et al., arXiv:2112.14589, Supplement, rearrangement paragraph); 4.3 µK during microwave operation at a reduced trap depth (Manetsch, "Qubit coherence"); Rb about 20 µK in the lattice (Chiu). The model scale (below 1 µK) is lower than every measured value: printed side by side, note: the measured values include heating in the trap.
Figures: `plot` series `motLoadFill` (N/N_ss against t/τ, two τ) and `pgcLimitVsDetuning` (T_lim against |δ|/γ at fixed s for c = 0.125 and 0.097, `yLines` at T_D and at the book's T_R).

## 3. O8 — One atom in a beam of light (links back: O5 `o5-dipole`, O5 `o5-lattice`)

**Apparatus.** A 1061 nm laser through an objective of NA 0.65 makes one focus per site, about 7.2 µm apart; an atom sits at the intensity maximum. **Measure.** Raman sidebands give the vibration frequencies; the trap lifetime is the loss of atoms with time. **Try it.** `tweezer-well`.
- `o8-beam` *The size of the focus.* *Set:* wavelength, NA, power. A Gaussian focus has waist w₀ and Rayleigh length z_R = πw₀²/λ; the diffraction scale is 0.61 λ/NA (Airy radius, stated). The paper does not print the Cs waist (research note, Gap); the reported radial and axial frequencies fix it: ω_r/ω_z = √2 π w₀/λ. *Claims:* `o8WaistUm` 1.24 µm (**our inference** from the two reported frequencies, labelled so on the page) [o8WaistFromRatio], `o8RayleighUm` 4.56 µm [o8Rayleigh], `o8AiryUm` 0.996 µm [o8DiffractionAiry], `o8SpacingOverWaist` 5.80 (7.2 µm over w₀) [o8SpacingOverWaist]. *Reported:* λ 1061 nm, NA 0.65, spacing about 7.2 µm, about 1.4 mW per tweezer (Manetsch, Methods "Tweezer generation", "Large-scale optical tweezer generation"); radial 29.30(4) kHz and axial 5.64(3) kHz from Raman sidebands at full depth (Manetsch; figure to be confirmed on the page image, the note names the paper only).
- `o8-depth` *How deep.* Far from both lines the light shift is the scalar polarizability times the intensity: U = −α I₀/2ε₀c with I₀ = 2P/πw₀². For cesium at 1061 nm both fine-structure lines count (weights 2 and 1 in the Γ form) with their counter-rotating terms. The book's near-resonance `dipoleDepth` is NOT used here (it ignores the second line), and the page says why in one sentence. *Claims:* `o8AlphaAu` 1,165 atomic units [o8Alpha] (D lines only; 1,152 au at 1064 nm, `o8AlphaAu1064`, against the Cs value in the literature of about 1.15×10³ au in our memory, unfetched: a test anchor, not a claim), `o8Depth14` 0.152 mK at 1.4 mW and w₀ 1.24 µm [o8DepthAt1.4mW], `o8DepthFromFreq` 0.208 mK (U = Mω_r²w₀²/4 from the reported radial frequency) [o8DepthFromFreq], `o8PowerForFreq` 1.92 mW [o8PowerForFreqDepth], `o8FrAt14` 25.0 kHz and `o8FzAt14` 4.81 kHz [fr, fz at 1.4 mW]. *Reported:* average depth k_B × 0.18(2) mK at full depth, about 1.4 mW at the atoms (Manetsch, same paragraphs): printed beside 0.152 and 0.208 mK, note: our depth uses our inferred waist and one power; the mean over 12,000 sites is not one tweezer.
- `o8-motion` *Cold enough to stay in the well.* Thermal rms size σ = √(k_BT/Mω_r²); Lamb–Dicke parameter η = k√(ħ/2Mω_r) for the 852 nm light (the photon that heats it); depth over temperature. *Claims:* `o8Sigma4` 89.1 nm and `o8Sigma20` 192 nm (T = 4.3 µK and 20 µK; the 4.3 µK is the reported figure used as input) [rms r], `o8Eta` 0.266 and `o8EtaSq` 0.0705 [o8LambDickeRad, o8EtaSq], `o8DepthOverT20` 7.59 (0.152 mK over 20 µK) [o8DepthOverTpgc]. Link `o5-lattice` (vibrational levels, Raman sidebands).
- `o8-heat` *What the trap light itself does.* Scattered trap light heats by one recoil pair per photon and is the floor under every lifetime: R = Σ_j (U_j/ħ)(γ_j/Δ_j) (rotating-wave, both lines). *Claims:* `o8Rsc` 1.45 s⁻¹ [o8Rsc], `o8RecoilGainK` 128 nK per scattered trap photon (2E_R/k_B at 1061 nm) [o8RecoilGainTrapK], `o8HeatTime` 819 s (depth over 2E_R·R; `recoilHeatingTime` with the trap photon's own recoil) [o8HeatTimeTrapK]. *Reported:* trap lifetime 22.9 min, from vacuum (Manetsch; page to be confirmed): shown beside 819 s = 13.7 min; note: different mechanisms, both far above one run of a second.
Figure: `plot` curve `tweezerU` (U(r)/k_B in mK against r in µm at w₀ 1.24 µm, 1.4 mW, with the harmonic approximation as a second series; `yLines` at the reported 0.18 mK as a hollow dashed line).

## 4. O9 — One atom per trap (links back: O8; O7 `o7-sequence`; forward: B6 loss as a third outcome, P2)

**Apparatus.** The same tweezers loaded from the cooled cloud with a short pulse of red-detuned light; a camera image finds the occupied sites; a moving tweezer rearranges them; a reservoir is refilled. **Measure.** The fraction of sites with one atom; how many sites fill a target. **Try it.** `parity-loading`, `sort-success`, `refill-rounds`.
- `o9-parity` *Why about half.* In a tweezer small enough for light-assisted collisions, pairs leave together: an even number of atoms ends as none, an odd number as one. With Poisson mean μ the chance of one is (1 − e^{−2μ})/2 (**a model**, labelled so; Monte Carlo of the pair loss agrees to 5×10⁻⁵ [MC parity]). *Claims:* `o9P1Mu1` 0.4323 and `o9P0Mu1` 0.5677 [mu=1], `o9PoissonMax` 0.3679 (best Poisson single-atom chance at μ = 1), `o9P1Mu2` 0.4908, `o9P1Mu5` 0.49998 [mu=2, mu=5]. *Reported:* 51.2 % over about 12,000 sites with 3.4 % relative spread across sites, 6,139 atoms on average (Manetsch Fig. 1d); about 0.6 (Bernien et al., arXiv:1707.04344, loading scheme differs); up to 40 % (Gyger et al., arXiv:2402.04994); above 50 % for about 70 repeats (Chiu, Ext. Data Fig. 4). The model's ceiling is exactly ½; the reported 51.2 % lies just above it: printed with the 3.4 % spread, neutral note (see Q4).
- `o9-sort` *Moving atoms into a target.* Imaging, then moves: a target of M sites can be filled when at least M of N sites loaded, a binomial tail. *Claims:* `o9Sites100` 229 sites for a 99 % chance to fill 100 at p = 0.512 and `o9Sites100p50` 235 at p = 0.5, `o9Sites50` 122 [needN], `o9PCert200` 0.659 and `o9PCert220` 0.962 [Bin(N, 0.512)], `o9ExpAtoms` 6,144 (12,000 × 0.512, next to the reported 6,139). *Reported (estimates, labelled as such):* parallel sort of the 12,000-site array about 137 ms and sequential about 522 ms (Manetsch, Outlook and SI Sec. II, SI Table I); rearrangement about 150 ms per cycle (Norcia et al., arXiv:2401.16177); 20–40 ms (Chiu).
- `o9-refill` *Keeping the array full.* Each cycle an atom is lost with chance ε and an empty site refilled with chance q: the empty fraction follows e′ = e(1−q) + (1−e)ε and settles at ε/(q+ε). With ε = 0.01 and q = 0.5 (**model inputs**, stated): *Claims:* `o9Empty5` 0.0473 after five rounds [round 5], `o9EmptyEq` 0.0196 [o9RefillEq], `o9AllFilled100` 0.138 (the chance that 100 sites at 98.04 % fill are all full: why one wants more than a fill fraction) [P(all 100)]. *Reported:* 98–99 % fill after reload (Norcia, Appendix); occupancy above 50 % kept for about 70 repeats (Chiu, Ext. Data Fig. 4).
- `o9-loss` *The price of an image.* Atoms lost per second is N/τ, atoms lost per image N(1 − S): with the reported inputs 6,139 atoms, vacuum lifetime 22.9 min and per-image survival 99.98952 % (Manetsch, Ext. Data Fig. 6; stated inputs): `o9LossPerSec` 4.47 s⁻¹ [o9LossPerSec], `o9LostPerImage` 0.643 [o9LostPerImage]; reload rate must match loss rate times array size (the rule behind continuous operation; Chiu, Gyger, Norcia: words). Link forward in words: B6 treats loss as a third outcome of a measurement (ref [2] of GANDALF).
Figure: `camera-frame` (§7) with `show: 'occupancy'` (one seeded shot, 8 × 8, p = 0.5, labelled "one random shot", no number printed from it); `plot` series `parityP1` and `poissonP1` with `yLines` [0.5] and the reported 0.512 as a hollow point.

## 5. O10 — Light from one atom (links back: O1 `o1-force`, O2 `o2-limit`, O8; forward: O11, B5)

**Apparatus.** Imaging beams on the cycling line at a detuning from resonance, a high-NA objective, a camera. **Measure.** The photons that reach the camera. **Try it.** `photon-budget`.
- `o10-rate` *How many photons an atom gives.* R = (γ/2) s/(1 + s + 4δ²/γ²): at most γ/2, at saturation and zero detuning γ/4. *Claims:* `o10RmaxCs` 1.64×10⁷ s⁻¹ [o10RmaxCs], `o10RSat` 8.22×10⁶ s⁻¹ (s = 1, δ = 0) [o10R_s1_d0], `o10RExample` 1.73×10⁶ s⁻¹ (s = 2, δ = −2γ; **setup values**, not a paper's) [o10Rexample], `o10RbAnchor` 1,733 photons in 1 ms at s = 0.1 on resonance (Rb; a test anchor against the paper's "about 2,000", not shown as a claim because the saturation-intensity conventions differ: Fuhrmanek et al., arXiv:1012.4387, p. 2). Reuses `scatterRate`.
- `o10-collect` *How many reach the camera.* An ideal lens of numerical aperture NA gathers (1 − √(1−NA²))/2 of isotropic light. *Claims:* `o10CollNa40` 4.17 %, `o10CollNa55` 8.24 %, `o10CollNa65` 12.0 %, `o10CollNa70` 14.3 % [o10Coll]. *Reported (all hollow points; the gap is the lesson):* total detection efficiency 0.6 % (Fuhrmanek, p. 2–3), 1.6–2.0 % (Kwon et al., arXiv:1706.09497, p. 2 and Suppl. p. 8; this paper also prints 4.17 % for isotropic emission at NA 0.4, our number matches), 0.96 % and 0.37 % (Chow et al., arXiv:2206.00144, pp. 3 and 5), 0.014 (Scott et al., arXiv:2507.01720, p. 3). The page lists what multiplies the ideal: optics, detector quantum efficiency, light outside the region of interest (words, as the papers decompose it).
- `o10-need` *How many photons make a bright atom.* Mean detected count m = R η τ. A bright atom shows zero counts with chance e^{−m}. *Claims:* `o10Floor5` 0.674 % and `o10Floor7` 0.0912 % [o10Floor], `o10NScat5` 521 and `o10NScat7` 729 scattered photons at η = 0.96 % (m = 5, 7) [N scat], `o10TauM5` 0.0578 ms for m = 5 at the example rate and η = 5 % [o10TauForM5]. The m ≥ 5 and m ≥ 7 rules are Gibbons et al., arXiv:1012.1682, p. 2 (reported rule, our arithmetic).
- `o10-limit` *Why not scatter more.* Each photon kicks the atom twice (absorbed, emitted), 2E_R = ħ²k²/M of kinetic energy, and the cooling light balances only part of it; each photon also has a small chance of dropping the atom into the wrong hyperfine level (depumping). *Claims:* `o10GainK` 198 nK per photon (Cs, 852 nm) [o10EnergyPerPhoton], `o10PhotonsToDepth` 908 (0.18 mK over that gain, no cooling) [o10PhotonsToDepth], `o10DepumpN` 2,703 photons at a depumping chance 3.7×10⁻⁴ per photon (**input**, the reported total of Chow's budget; page to be confirmed) [o10NforOneOverE], `o10Surv500` 0.831 [survive N=500]. **Not shown:** the 720 nK per photon for Rb and the 180 µK for 250 photons that Gibbons (p. 2) quotes: our energy per photon is 362 nK (Rb) and 250 photons give 90 µK; the two differ by a factor 2 in what "T_recoil" means, undecided until the paper's page is read (Q3).
Figures: `plot` points figure `collectionVsNA` (curve, ideal, with the reported efficiencies as hollow points at the NA each paper states) and curve `detectedVsExposure`.

## 6. O11 — Counting atoms (links back: O10; Q25 `q25-readout`; forward: B5 filters, B6 readout time, P2 Fig. 2 and Table 1)

**Apparatus.** The camera of O10; for one site, the counts in a region of interest in a bright shot (atom) and a dark shot (none). **Measure.** Histograms; the fraction of sites correct; the fraction of atoms still there. **Try it.** `count-histograms`, `exposure-optimum`.
- `o11-hist` *Two Poisson histograms and a cutoff.* Bright mean m_b = signal + background, dark mean m_d = background; a count at or above n_c reads "atom"; the error is half the sum of the two misreads. Q25 `q25-readout` taught this with means 8.2 and 0.2; here the means come from O10's budget. *Claims:* `o11FuhrErr` 0.323 % at n_c = 3, `o11FuhrEb` 0.531 %, `o11FuhrEd` 0.115 % (means 9.2 and 0.2) [o11Fuhr]. *Reported:* the paper's measured error 1.2 % at n_c = 2 (Fuhrmanek, Fig. 2–3, Table I): printed beside the Poisson floor, note: the quoted means alone give 0.32 %, the paper's own error budget names detector dark counts; no verdict. Link `qc-o-q25-readout` (new row).
- `o11-exposure` *More light, fewer errors.* Means grow as τ; the separation of the two histograms in standard deviations grows as √τ (four times the exposure doubles it: `o11DprimeRatio` 2.00 [d prime]). With a bright rate 54.4 kcps and a background 0.52 kcps (the inputs of Chow's Fig.; reported inputs): `o11Err50` 4.49 %, `o11Err100` 1.40 %, `o11Err150` 0.267 %, `o11Err260` 0.0196 %, `o11Err350` 0.00166 % (50, 100, 150, 260, 350 µs, best integer cutoff each) [o11Chow]. *Reported:* ideal errors 0.005 % and 0.04 % at 260 µs (Chow, the dark and bright misreads, p. 3 of the note; mean 0.0225 % matches our 0.0196 % to within the figure's reading); GANDALF's own time axis (Fig. 2, letters) is P2's. Link forward in words: B5 replaces the cutoff by weights over many pixels.
- `o11-camera` *The camera's own noise.* An EMCCD multiplies each photoelectron by a random gain (Erlang with shape n and scale g) and adds Gaussian read noise: for Poisson n the variance is exactly 2·mean·g², an "excess noise factor" of 2 (`o11Enf` 2.00 [o11ENFvar 1.997 Monte Carlo]). For a 30-photoelectron bright atom against 0.3 dark, gain 300, read noise 40 counts (setup values): `o11EmccdErr` 0.0360 % at the best cutoff [o11EmccdErr] against `o11EmccdFloor` 7.5×10⁻⁶ % for the same means counted photon by photon [Poisson floor]: the gain's spread, not the photons, sets the error. *Reported:* camera parameters and the model are from Martinez-Dorantes et al., arXiv:1706.00264, Suppl. pp. 5–7 (ref [27] in the notes) and Kwon, Suppl. pp. 6–7 (words, no number).
- `o11-loss` *The best exposure.* Longer exposure reads better and loses more atoms. Toy model, labelled so: misread error from `o11-exposure` plus a loss chance 1 − e^{−λ_L τ}; at λ_L = 4 s⁻¹ (**toy input**) the total is smallest at `o11ToyTau` 234 µs, total 0.120 %, of which misread 0.0264 % [o11ToyOptimumTau, min total, misread at opt]. The quantity GANDALF plots is fidelity times survival: with the reported inputs (Manetsch, Ext. Data Fig. 6) 99.99374 % × 99.98952 % at 80 ms gives `o11FS80` 0.999833, and 99.9571 % × 99.176 % at 20 ms gives `o11FS20` 0.991335 [o9FidSurvProd]. Link forward in words: B6's U-shaped readout-time optimum in the error-correction cycle.
Figures: `plot` series `errVsExposure` {rb, rg} (log y), `camera-frame` with `show: 'counts'`, `noise: 'emccd'`, 6 × 6 (one seeded frame, no number read from it).
