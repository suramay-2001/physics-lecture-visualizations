### Unit `b6-twirl` — Waiting costs: idle error as three chances
**b1 [B]** what waiting does to a qubit · G: a waiting qubit slowly leans to its resting state and slowly forgets its phase; these have two clocks. · F: amplitude damping with time T₁ shrinks the Bloch z-component by e^{−t/T₁} and pulls it to rest; dephasing with T₂ shrinks x and y by e^{−t/T₂} (Q13 Kraus maps; `[38]` Eq. 7). Stage `pl(shrink factors vs t)`. Link Q13.
**b2 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-pauli-twirl']`** twirling · G: replace the messy leaning by the nearest "error is X, Y, Z or nothing with fixed chances" model. · F: averaging the channel over Pauli conjugations keeps only its diagonal in the Pauli basis; the result is a Pauli channel (Q21 `q21-digitize`: any error is a mix of four). Words + `pl`.
**b3 [B]** the three chances (D1) · F: `[38]` Eqs. 9–10: p_X = p_Y = (1 − e^{−t/T₁})/4, p_Z = (1 − e^{−t/T₂})/2 − p_X. For t = 4 ms: p_X = p_Y `b6PxPy` 9.998×10⁻⁵, p_Z `b6Pz` 1.896×10⁻³, total `b6IdleTotal4ms` 2.096×10⁻³; Z dominates by `b6PzOverPx` 19×. Stage `pl(p_X,p_Y,p_Z vs t)`. Reported `tom-eq9`.
**b4 [B]** the straight-line rule · G: for short waits each chance grows in proportion to the time. · F: total ≈ t/(4T₁) + t/(2T₂) = `b6IdleSmallT` 0.0021, exact/approx `b6IdleSmallRatio` 0.998; the paper's pessimism: twirling overstates logical error compared with full damping (`[38]` abstract). Stage `pl(exact, line)`.
**b5 [C]** clue: p_Z in percent for t = 50 ms, T₁ = 5 s, T₂ = 0.5 s; idle total for T₁ = ∞ (§4).
**b6 [D]** what the paper does not print · F: GANDALF §7.2 p. 12 uses this model "characterized by T₁ and T₂" but prints no values, no gate errors, and no rule turning classifier error into a flip chance (P2 lists it as open); every curve here uses our toy values. Words.
### Unit `b6-ucurve` — Reading error against waiting error
**b1 [B]** two errors pull opposite ways · G: a longer look reads more reliably but leaves every data atom waiting longer; add the two and the total has a lowest point. · F: p_eff(t) = p_g + p_idle(t) + p_m(t); p_m falls steeply (Poisson overlap, O11: error from the best cutoff on counts 6t and 0.3t): 2.714 %, 0.282 %, 4.2×10⁻³ % at 1, 2, 4 ms (`b6PmAt1ms/2ms/4ms`); p_idle rises about 0.05 % per ms. Stage `pl(p_m, p_idle, p_eff vs t, log y)`. Link `o11-exposure`, `o11-loss`.
**b2 [B]** the minimum · G: the best reading time sits where one more millisecond buys no more than it costs. · F: p_eff = 0.5873 %, 0.4138 %, 0.7225 % at 2, 4, 10 ms (`b6PeffAt1/2/4/10`); minimum 0.3906 % at t* = 2.95 ms on a 0.05 ms grid, 2.97 ms by bounded minimisation (`b6TStar`, `b6TStarRefined`). Toy: not fitted to the paper, whose best is "about 4 ms" at 9 µm (reported `g-best`); positions prove nothing. Stage `pl` + marker at t*.
**b3 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-ucurve']`** why the exponent (d+1)/2 (D2) · G: a failure needs more than half of d guards wrong at once; (d+1)/2 wrong guards each costing p gives p^((d+1)/2). · F: cheapest failing fault set has k = (d+1)/2 faults (d odd); repetition vote exact `b6Rep3`…`b6Rep13`, leading-term ratio 0.9993, 0.9985, 0.9976, 0.9957, 0.9948 for d = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 at p = 10⁻³. Stage `pl(exact vs leading term)`. Link `b1-threshold` (published fits).
**b4 [B]** the exponent amplifies the valley, not its place · G: raising d makes the dip deeper and the walls steeper, but the lowest point stays put. · F: p_L = A(p_eff/p_th)^k is a monotone map of p_eff, so argmin is the same for every d (route-checked d = 3, 11, 25, `b6ArgminSameD`). Missing t* by a factor 2 costs ×`b6Amp2tstarD3` 1.70 at d = 3 and ×`b6Amp2tstarD11` 4.90 at d = 11. p_L(t*, d = 11) = `b6PLStarD11` 1.07×10⁻⁴; at 10 ms `b6PLAt10msD11` 4.27×10⁻³. Where p_eff ≥ p_th (here at 1 ms, where p_eff = 2.97 %) the formula does not apply: the stage greys that range. Stage `pl(p_L vs t, d = 3, 11, 25; log y)`.
**b5 [D]** mixed fault rates · F: with n locations of unequal rates, the leading term is e_k(p₁…p_n), the elementary symmetric polynomial, bounded by C(n,k)·p̄^k (Maclaurin), equality for equal rates. For 5 locations at 0.001 and 6 at 0.005, k = 6: e₆ `b6E6` 2.300×10⁻¹³ against the bound `b6MaclaurinBound` 4.794×10⁻¹³ (`b6MaclaurinRatio` 0.480); exact failure `b6MixedExact` 2.278×10⁻¹³. So "p_eff = a simple average" is a bound, not the model; our toy sums rates and is labelled so (N3). Words + `pl`.
**b6 [C]** clue: exponent for d = 15; factor when p_eff doubles at d = 7; vote error d = 3, p = 0.1 (§4).
### Unit `b6-pipelining` — Hiding the classical work
**b1 [B]** serial rounds · G: read, then classify, then the next round: every step waits for the one before. · F: T_serial = d(g + r + c): gates g, acquisition r, classification c, d rounds. d = 11, g = 0.02, r = 2, c = 0.5 ms: `b6Serial` 27.72 ms. Stage `tl(serial)`.
**b2 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-pipelined']`** overlapped rounds (D3) · G: classify round k while round k+1 is already being gated and photographed; only the last classification adds time. · F: T_pipe = (g + r) + c + (d−1)·max(g + r, c). Same inputs: `b6Pipe` 22.72 ms, saving `b6Saving` (d−1)c = 5.00 ms (route: event simulation, equal to 1e-12). Stage `tl(pipelined)`.
**b3 [B]** a slow classifier · G: if classification is slower than a round, it becomes the bottleneck and queues up. · F: for c > g + r the pipeline runs at one round per c: T = (g + r) + d·c. g = 0.02, r = 0.3, c = 0.5: serial `b6SerialSlow` 9.02, pipelined `b6PipeSlowClassifier` 5.82; overlap stops helping at c = `b6BreakEvenC` g + r. Stage `tl` + `pl(T vs c)`.
**b4 [B]** what overlap needs · F: the next round's gates must not depend on this round's classification (decoding is deferred through the Pauli frame, B1); and atom reload must wait one round (unit 5). P2's Eq. 5, d(t_r + t_g) + (t_den + t_c), is this formula with c = t_den + t_c when c ≤ g + r (route check). Link P2 `p2-pipeline`; words only about the paper's numbers.
**b5 [C]** clue: serial and pipelined time for d = 9, g = 0.01, r = 1, c = 0.4; the same for c = 1.5; the break-even (§4).
### Unit `b6-loss-reset` — Atoms that leave; reset or not
**b1 [B]** three outcomes · G: reading can say "bright", "dark" or "gone". A classifier trained only on bright and dark calls a gone atom dark. · F: `[2]` §2: state-selective readout separates |0⟩, |1⟩ and loss; a delayed-erasure decoder then uses the loss flags. GANDALF's dataset labels are bright/dark (P2 lists this as a gap, derived, not the paper's claim). Stage `bits(atoms with lost marked)`.
**b2 [B]** how many atoms leave · G: with 241 atoms and 1 loss in 10 000 per image, one atom is lost about every 40 rounds. · F: reported survival per image 99.98952 % (Manetsch via O9 `o9-loss`) gives 1 − S = 1.048×10⁻⁴; surface code d = 11 has 2d²−1 = `b6AtomsD11` 241 atoms: `b6LossPerRound` 0.0253 per round, `b6LossPer11Rounds` 0.278, no loss in 11 rounds `b6NoLoss11Rounds` 0.757, one loss per `b6RoundsToOneLoss` 39.6 rounds. Stage `pl(P(no loss) vs rounds)`. Reported input only.
**b3 [B]** a known place is cheaper than an unknown error · G: if you know which guard is gone you can still vote with the rest; only losing all guards fails. · F: repetition code, d bits, erasure chance e, no flips: failure needs all d erased (then a coin flip): ½e^d, `b6ErasureRep5` 5×10⁻¹¹ at d = 5, e = 0.01, against the unknown-flip vote needing (d+1)/2 flips: cheapest failing set 11 erasures against 6 flips at d = 11 (`b6MinFaultsErasure11`, `b6MinFaultsUnknown11`). Standard counting, ours. Stage `pl(erasure vs flip)`. Reported `[2]` thresholds: about 7.2 % loss threshold with perfect loss information, about 2.3 % for the SWAP scheme; 1.5–2.5 % at a loss fraction near one half (p. 7–8).
**b4 [B]** reset or no reset · G: skipping reset saves time but a wrong reading can leave a trace in the next one. · F: `[11]`: for memory, reset gives no benefit; for logical operations, no reset can double the time-like cost unless reset is slow or noisy: no reset wins above about 100 ns reset time and physical error about 10^−2.5 in their superconducting-like model (abstract, §I–II). For atoms reload takes ms, so their threshold does not transfer; GANDALF uses `[2]` and `[11]` as licence to defer reload by one round (§5.3 p. 11), which is motivation, not proof (N4). Reported only. Words + `rp`.
**b5 [C]** clue: expected atom losses per 100 rounds at d = 7, loss 10⁻⁴ per image; chance in 10⁻⁶ that a 5-bit erasure vote fails at e = 0.1 (§4).
**b6 [D]** read-out induced loss · F: faster, brighter imaging raises heating and loss (experiment track O10, O11); B6 only notes the coupling, with the track's toy `o11-loss`. Link forward to P2 `p2-pipeline`. Words.

## 2. Derivations (step `tex` — why — **view**; Ground ≥ Formal; ≥ 2 distinct views per track)
**D1 · twirl:b3 · the three idle chances from the Bloch vector** — G (4): (1) a waiting qubit's arrow shrinks: z by e^{−t/T₁}, x and y by e^{−t/T₂} · **pl shrink factors** · (2) a model "X with chance a, Y with b, Z with c" shrinks the arrow too: z by 1 − 2(a+b), x by 1 − 2(b+c), y by 1 − 2(a+c) · **pl bars of a, b, c** · (3) match z: a + b = (1 − e^{−t/T₁})/2, and by symmetry a = b · **pl** · (4) match x: c = (1 − e^{−t/T₂})/2 − b · **pl p vs t**. F (3): (1) Pauli channel with (p_X, p_Y, p_Z) has transfer factors λ_x = 1 − 2(p_Y+p_Z), λ_y = 1 − 2(p_X+p_Z), λ_z = 1 − 2(p_X+p_Y) · **bars** · (2) set λ_z = e^{−t/T₁}, λ_x = λ_y = e^{−t/T₂}; since λ_x = λ_y, p_X = p_Y · **pl** · (3) solve: `[38]` Eqs. 9–10 (route: Kraus products and the χ diagonal agree) · **pl p vs t**. The twirl drops the part that pulls z towards rest (non-unital), which is why it is pessimistic.
**D2 · ucurve:b3 · why (d+1)/2** — G (4): (1) d guards vote; the code fails when more than half are wrong · **pl vote bars** · (2) the cheapest way: exactly (d+1)/2 wrong; the number of ways is C(d,(d+1)/2) · **pl** · (3) each way has chance p^((d+1)/2) · **pl exact vs leading term** · (4) with p = 10⁻³ the leading term is within 0.5 % of the exact vote up to d = 13 · **pl ratio**. F (3): (1) P_fail = Σ_{k ≥ (d+1)/2} C(d,k)p^k(1−p)^{d−k} · **pl** · (2) leading term C(d,(d+1)/2)p^((d+1)/2); the ratio is `b6RepRatio…` · **pl ratio** · (3) unequal rates: leading term e_k(p), at most C(n,k)p̄^k (Maclaurin) · **pl mixed**. Honest label: the surface code's prefactor A comes from fits (B1), not from this derivation; the exponent counts faults of any kind (data, gate, reading) alike.
**D3 · pipelining:b2 · the overlapped schedule** — G (3): (1) round k is gated and photographed in time g + r, back to back · **tl rows** · (2) the classifier starts when the photo is done and the previous job is finished; each job lasts c · **tl** · (3) all rounds done when the last job finishes: (g + r) + c + (d−1)·max(g + r, c) · **tl + pl T vs c**. F (3): (1) acquisition ends at k(g+r); (2) job k ends at max(k(g+r), end of job k−1) + c · **tl** · (3) induction gives the closed form; saving over serial is (d−1)c when c ≤ g + r · **pl**.
**viewCaptions:** D1 "shrink of x, y, z" / "chances a, b, c" / "chances against t"; D2 "vote and failures" / "exact against leading term" / "mixed rates"; D3 "rows of the schedule" / "time against classifier time".
