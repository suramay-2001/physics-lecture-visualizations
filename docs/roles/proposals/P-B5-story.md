### Unit `b5-roi` — A spot on a grid of pixels
**b1 [B]** the smear · G: one atom's light lands as a soft spot over several pixels; each pixel counts photons by chance. · F: detected photons spread by the PSF (here a Gaussian) and are Poisson counted per pixel; pixel share = difference of two error functions per axis. Stage `cf(sites:[[2,2]], photons:20, psf:0.9, background:0.4, noise:'none', show:'photons')` (means, no noise). Claims `b5PsfCenter` 0.1776, `b5PsfEdge` 0.1018, `b5PsfCorner` 0.0583 · Trail `[phu24 §III: dataset, spot sizes]`.
**b2 [B]** the region of interest · G: add up the pixels in a box around the site; a 3×3 box holds 82 % of the light, a 5×5 box 99 %. · F: ROI sum S = Σ_{ij∈ROI} n_ij; mean light in box `b5PsfRoi3` 0.8180, `b5PsfRoi5` 0.9891 of N. Stage `cf(… roi:{size:3}, show:'photons')`. Link O10 (the photon budget gave N).
**b3 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-roi']`** a count in a box, a threshold · G: call the atom "there" if the box total reaches a cut. · F: bright mean m₁ = b|ROI| + N·f, dark m₀ = b|ROI|; the box total is Poisson(m) (sum of Poisson counts), so O11's `thresholdError` applies unchanged: square 3×3 `b5ErrSquare3` 0.456 % at the best cut (exact Poisson sum `b5SquarePoisson`). Stage `cf(counts, noise:'poisson', seed:7)` + `pl(statistic pmfs, step)`. Link `o11-hist`.
**b4 [B]** a neighbour's light · G: a second bright atom two pixels away leaks light into your box; four pixels away it hardly does. · F: fraction of one atom's light inside a ±1-pixel window around a neighbour at separation Δ: `b5CrossFrac2` 0.2892, `b5CrossFrac4` 0.0027. Stage `cf(sites:[[2,2],[2,4]], show:'photons')`; reported marks: spacing 5 µm gives crosstalk, 9 µm little (PHU §III–IV), images 28×28 and 32×32 (GDF p. 11, P2 owns the dataset).
**b5 [C]** clue: how much of the light falls outside a 3×3 box for σ = 0.6 and σ = 1.2? (answers in §4).
**b6 [D]** why the spot is Gaussian · F: diffraction gives an Airy pattern, whose core a Gaussian approximates; the choice only shapes weights and is a modelling input (O10 owns the optics). Words only.
### Unit `b5-matched-filter` — Weighting pixels: the best sum
**b1 [B]** one pixel's evidence · G: a pixel with 3 counts where a bright atom adds 1.2 on a background 0.4 is strong evidence for "bright". · F: LLR of one pixel = −s + n ln(1 + s/b); for n = 3, s = 1.2, b = 0.4 it is `b5LlrOnePixel` 2.9589. Stage `pl(llrVsCount)` (line n ↦ −s + n ln(1+s/b)). Derivation D1.
**b2 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-llr-weights']`** the weights · G: add every pixel's evidence: each count times its own weight, minus a fixed amount. · F: LLR = Σ w_i n_i − Σ s_i with w_i = ln(1 + s_i/b_i); the bit is "bright" when LLR > 0, i.e. S = Σ w_i n_i exceeds Σ s_i = `b5LlrThreshold` 16.36 (3×3, our inputs). Stage `cf(show:'weights', filter:'llr')`: weights `b5WLlr` 2.291 (centre), 1.806 (edge), 1.365 (corner). Claims as listed.
**b3 [B]** three sums compared · G: the best sum makes fewer mistakes than a flat box or a Gaussian-shaped sum, and all beat reading one pixel. · F: exact errors, equal priors, our inputs: centre pixel `b5ErrCenter` 7.83 %, Gaussian-shaped (w ∝ s) `b5ErrGauss` 0.487 %, flat 3×3 `b5ErrSquare3` 0.457 %, likelihood ratio `b5ErrLlr` 0.416 %, equal to the Bayes error by full enumeration `b5ErrBayes` (the LLR rule *is* the minimum). A Monte Carlo of the nine pixels agrees (`b5ErrLlrMC` 0.426 %, 400 000 frames, 4σ band). Here the flat box beats the Gaussian shape because s/b reaches 8.9; weights ∝ s are the weak-signal form (s ≪ b), see D1. Stage `pl(series classifierErr: square, gauss, llr)` and `cf(show:'weights')` ×3 panels.
**b4 [B]** exposure and the square root · G: double the light and the background together and the separation grows by √2; four times the exposure doubles it. · F: d′² = Σ s_i²/b_i in the weak-signal limit (`b5Dprime2` 86.6 for our inputs), so scaling s, b by τ gives d′ ∝ √τ (`b5DprimeRatio4` 2.00). Likelihood-ratio error: 3.11 % at τ = ½, 0.416 % at 1, 0.0095 % at 2 (`b5ErrLlrTau0.5`, `b5ErrLlrTau2.0`); the flat box at τ = 2 gives 0.0124 % (`b5ErrSquare3Tau2`). Stage `pl(errVsTau)`. Link back O11 `o11-exposure`; the Gaussian estimate `b5NormalErr` 1.6×10⁻⁶ is far below the true 0.42 %: **Poisson tails are fatter than the normal's; use the exact sum** (neutral note N2).
**b5 [C]** clue: weight of a pixel with s = 3, b = 1; d′² for two pixels; what ×9 exposure does to d′ (§4).
**b6 [D]** the ceiling · F: a classifier sees only the pixels, so none beats the Bayes error of the true image model; what denoise-then-classify can gain is information the sum ignores (crosstalk, atom loss, non-Poisson camera noise), or a better prior. Words + `b5ErrBayes`.
### Unit `b5-cnn` — Learning the weights, then a small network
**b1 [B]** learn instead of derive · G: if you do not know the spot, collect labelled shots and fit the weights by least squares. · F: KEN Eq. A1: W = Y Xᵀ(XXᵀ + αI)⁻¹ with X flattened pixels in a box plus a constant, Y the 0/1 labels from the long exposure; the fit is convex. Stage `cf(show:'weights', filter:'learned')`. Derivation D2 (population version, closed form).
**b2 [B]** an isolated atom: the learned filter is s/(b + ps) · F: with prior p = ½, Cov(n) = diag(μ) + p(1−p) ssᵀ, μ = b + ps; Sherman–Morrison gives W ∝ s_i/μ_i (route check `ok`, `b5Q` 33.89, shrink `b5LsShrink` 0.894). G: the fitted weights look like the spot divided by the local brightness. Stage `pl(weightProfile iso)`.
**b3 [B]** a bright neighbour gets a negative weight · G: when a second atom sits two pixels away, the filter learns to subtract the pixels that belong to it. · F: 7-pixel strip, atoms at 2 and 4, σ = 0.9, p = ½: weights (relative to the largest) 0.62, 1.00, 0.96, 0.41, **−0.13, −0.23, −0.15** (`b5XtWeights`); without the neighbour the same pixels are positive (`b5XtIso`). A sampled regression (600 000 shots) agrees to 0.03. Stage `pl(weightProfile iso vs xt)` + `cf(sites:[[0,2],[0,4]], show:'weights', filter:'learned')`. Reported: KEN's MF-Array shows its largest negative weights on neighbours (§IV, Fig. 5, text).
**b4 [B]** what a CNN adds · G: a small network can bend the rule (a nonlinear step) and look at neighbours at once; the price is many parameters. · F: PHU's per-site CNN: three valid 3×3 convolutions (32, 64, 128 filters) on 10×10 pixels, a dense layer of 128 and 2 outputs: our count from the listed layers `b5CnnSiteParams` 355 202 = the printed total; receptive field of three 3×3 layers `b5RfCnn` 7 pixels. Reported: PHU Table II array model 74 782 034 parameters; KEN: the matched filter matches the CNN's infidelity with about 100× fewer parameters and 10⁴× fewer multiplications, 3.5 % more error for the array version (abstract). Stage `cf(show:'rf', layers:[3,3,3])` (receptive field footprint from the engine).
**b5 [C]** clue: parameters of one 3×3 conv layer, 16 → 32 channels, with bias; receptive field of four 3×3 layers; ratio of learned weights at two pixels (§4).
**b6 [D]** metrics · F: fidelity F = 1 − ½[P(read bright | dark) + P(read dark | bright)]; cross-fidelity F_CF measures errors that co-occur between sites; reduction η = 1 − (1−F)/(1−F_base), **where the baseline differs by paper** (Gaussian mask in PHU/KEN, CNN(Site) in the GANDALF paper): Rosetta line R1 (§7).
### Unit `b5-wiener` — Cleaning a picture with a linear filter
**b1 [B]** smooth, then read the centre · G: blur the frame, then read the middle pixel: that is the same as one weighted sum. · F: for a linear map H, (Hn)_c = Σ_j H_cj n_j, a row of weights; blur kernel (¼, ½, ¼) then centre has weights (¼, ½, ¼). Stage `cf(panels:3: raw, blurred, weights)`. Claim `b5-w-centre` 0.5 (§4).
**b2 [B]** how good is each linear pipeline · G: on the same frame, a blur then centre pixel is worse than the best weights; a "smart" linear cleaner at best equals them. · F: Gaussian-approximation separation d² = Δᵀ Σ⁻¹ Δ for the output of H: raw (best weights) `b5D2Raw` 33.89; Wiener smoother `b5D2Wiener` 33.89 (equal: its rank-one output is the matched statistic); blur-then-centre `b5D2BlurCentre` 29.19 (`b5BlurFrac` 0.861); crop to 3 pixels 31.97; centre pixel alone 15.40. Stage `pl(bars d2)` + `cf`.
**b3 [B] · notation `introduces: ['pp-wiener']`** the Wiener smoother · G: it shrinks each pixel towards what the prior says a clean frame looks like. · F: x̂ = C_s(C_s + C_n)⁻¹(x − μ) + μ with C_s = p(1−p)ssᵀ (one atom, prior p) and C_n = diag(μ). Rank one: x̂ − μ = s·(matched-filter number); the cleaner *is* the matched filter times a template (route check). Shrink `b5LsShrink` 0.894. Stage `cf(panels:3: raw, wiener, truth, denoise:'wiener')` on one seeded frame (illustrative, labelled).
**b4 [B]** why a smoother then a threshold adds nothing · G: two linear steps make one linear step; a threshold on it is a threshold on a weighted sum, which the best weights already beat. · F: data processing: H ∘ (linear statistic) = (Hᵀu)ᵀx; no u can beat the Bayes weights of §2, equality when Hᵀu spans them. So the paper's claim that Gaussian, median and Wiener filters blur at low photon counts is a statement about *fixed* filters and a *nonlinear* downstream task, not shown by the paper (neutral note N3). Words, `b5D2*`.
**b5 [C]** clue: Wiener shrink for q = 3; kernel weight at the centre (§4).
### Unit `b5-pix2pix` — Picture-to-picture learning, and its risks
**b1 [B]** the idea · G: show a network pairs (noisy picture, clean picture) and train it to turn one into the other; a second network judges whether a clean picture is real. · F: conditional GAN G: x ↦ y with a discriminator D(x, y); objective L = L_GAN + λ L1 (ISO Eqs. 1–4, λ = 100 there; GDF Eq. 3, λ_L1 = 200). Stage `cf(panels:3: noisy, G(x), target)` (illustrative simulated pair). Reported `iso-obj`, `gdf-lambda`.
**b2 [B]** the L1 term and the GAN term · G: the L1 term keeps the picture close to the target on average; the adversarial term makes it sharp. · F: with the reported mean L1 0.0362 (GDF Table 3), λ L1 = `b5L1Scaled` 7.24 against a GAN term near ln 2 = `b5GanEq` 0.693 at D = ½: the L1 term sets the scale. Fake sample scored 0.2: −ln 0.2 = `b5GanFake02` 1.609. Stage `pl(points terms)`.
**b3 [B]** U-Net, patches, and the printed parameter count · G: skip links carry fine detail past the narrow middle; the judge looks at small patches. · F: PatchGAN with five 4×4 layers (strides 2, 2, 2, 1, 1) sees a 70×70 patch (`b5Rf70` 70, ISO §3.2.2). GDF Table 2's generator: our weight count 4 695 744 with two 3×3 convolutions per residual block (2 926 272 with one) against the printed 4.7 million (`b5GenParams2`, `b5GenParams1`); its discriminator of four stride-2 3×3 layers sees 31 pixels (`b5RfGdfD`) (neutral note N4: table does not say whether the output is patchwise). Stage `cf(show:'rf')`.
**b4 [B]** label smoothing · G: tell the judge "real = 0.9" so it does not grow overconfident. · F: with soft target 0.9 the cross-entropy bottoms out at H(0.9) = `b5SmoothFloor` 0.325, not 0. Reported `gdf-smooth` (p. 8–9), also fewer discriminator steps, dropout, cosine schedule, early stopping on validation L1.
**b5 [B/D] · `B5:hallucination`** a prior can invent an atom · G: if the network saw atoms in most training shots, a faint frame looks like an atom to it. · F: Bayes: posterior odds = likelihood ratio × prior odds. A frame with LLR = −1: posterior bright `b5PostEqual` 0.269 at prior ½, `b5PostPrior90` 0.768 at prior 0.9 (flip when the prior exceeds `b5FlipPrior` 0.731); occupancy moving from 50 % to 10 % shifts the log-odds by ln 9 = `b5ShiftLogit` 2.197. A *toy argument*, not a measurement of the paper's denoiser. Reported: GDF §4.3 p. 8 names hallucinated or erased sites, mode collapse and noise-pattern memorisation as risks and reports no measured rate (note N5). Stage `pl(posteriorVsPrior)`.
**b6 [C]** clue: GAN term for D = 0.25; λ L1 at mean L1 0.02; posterior for LLR = −2 at prior 0.8 (§4).

## 2. Derivations (step `tex` — why — **view**; Ground ≥ Formal; ≥ 2 views each)
**D1 · matched-filter:b2 · the Poisson likelihood-ratio weights** — G (4): (1) one pixel: chance of count n with mean m is mⁿe^{−m}/n!; compare bright mean b+s with dark mean b · **pl pmfs of one pixel** · (2) the ratio is e^{−s}(1+s/b)ⁿ: each count multiplies the odds by (1+s/b) · **pl ln-ratio vs n** · (3) pixels are independent, so the ratios multiply; take logs and the evidence adds: Σ n_i ln(1+s_i/b_i) − Σ s_i · **cf weights panel** · (4) when s ≪ b, ln(1+s/b) ≈ s/b: weights follow the spot, a Gaussian mask; when s ≫ b the log flattens the centre, so the flat box does well · **pl w vs s/b with the two lines**. F (3): (1) ln[P₁(n)/P₀(n)] = −Σs_i + Σ n_i ln(1+s_i/b_i) · **cf weights** · (2) equal priors: decide bright when this is positive; it minimises the error among all rules (Neyman–Pearson / Bayes) · **pl errors** · (3) enumeration of the three symmetry classes gives the minimum `b5ErrBayes` = `b5ErrLlr` · **pl bars**. Honest label: this derivation is ours (standard detection theory); neither KEN, PHU nor the paper states it.
**D2 · cnn:b2 · why the learned filter is s/(b+ps)** — G (3): (1) pixel counts have mean μ = b + p s and covariance with an extra bump from "atom or not" · **pl weightProfile** · (2) least squares asks for the combination of pixels that best predicts the label · **cf weights** · (3) solve: the answer is the spot divided by the brightness per pixel; a second atom changes μ and its pixels turn negative · **pl iso vs xt**. F (3): (1) Cov(n) = D + p(1−p)ssᵀ, D = diag(μ); Cov(n, a) = p(1−p)s · **pl** · (2) Sherman–Morrison: W = D⁻¹s·p(1−p)/(1 + p(1−p)q), q = sᵀD⁻¹s · **cf** · (3) with a neighbour s₂, solve the 2-term covariance; sign of the weight at the neighbour's centre is negative (`b5XtWeights`) · **pl**.
